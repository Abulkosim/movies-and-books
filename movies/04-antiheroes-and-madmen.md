# Lone Antiheroes & Madmen

The films here all stage the same problem: a man the world has refused to hold, who responds with violence, performance, or both. They are not character studies of "evil" — they are diagnoses of what the social order produces and then disowns.

---

## Joker (2019)

**What it's really about** — A society's elaborate machinery for ignoring the people it failed, told from the perspective of the failed person. The film's accomplishment is that it never asks you to like Arthur, only to admit that nothing kept him from becoming this.

**Key scenes**
- The bathroom dance after the subway killings — the first time he is alone with the relief of having acted.
- The mother-and-son revelation in the hospital.
- The Murray Franklin show monologue.
- The dance on the steps.

**Easy to miss** — Sophie. The neighbor relationship is one of Arthur's hallucinations almost from the start. The clue is the comedy-club scene where he applauds at the wrong moments — the same clip he replays of his "girlfriend." His perception is unreliable from frame one.

**The test** — Is the riot Arthur's victory or his irrelevance?

**Connections** — *Taxi Driver*, *Fight Club*, *The Dark Knight*, *American Psycho*.

## Taxi Driver (1976)

**What it's really about** — A veteran's loneliness rebranded as moral mission. Travis is not heroic; he is sick. The film's final irony is that the city, having no idea what to do with him, decides he is.

**Key scenes**
- "You talkin' to me?"
- The diary voiceover.
- The political-rally near-assassination.
- The brothel massacre.
- The newspaper-clippings denouement.

**Easy to miss** — The ending is ambiguous — many critics read it as Travis's dying fantasy. The fast-cut glance in the rearview mirror at the end is Scorsese signaling we are not safe; the next Travis is already getting in the cab.

**The test** — Is Travis a hero?

**Connections** — *Joker*, *Drive*, *American Psycho*.

## Fight Club (1999)

**What it's really about** — Not a celebration of nihilism. A diagnosis of late-90s consumerist masculinity that audiences turned into a brand. The Narrator's "freedom" through Tyler is a relapse, not a recovery.

**Key scenes**
- The IKEA-furniture spinning shot.
- "The first rule of Fight Club…"
- The chemical-burn kiss.
- The shooting-his-own-mouth ending and "you met me at a very strange time in my life."

**Easy to miss** — Marla is the protagonist's only real path out and the movie keeps treating her as background. The whole film is a man's attempt to be loved in a way that does not require him to actually meet someone.

**The test** — Why does the Narrator have to shoot himself in the face to become a person?

**Connections** — *American Psycho*, *Joker*, *The Social Network*.

## American Psycho (2000)

**What it's really about** — Not whether Bateman did the murders. The film is about the interchangeability of the men around him. They mistake him for other men, can't keep his name straight, and don't care when he confesses. He is unreal because his class made him unreal.

**Key scenes**
- The business-card scene.
- The "Hip to Be Square" Paul-Allen-axe.
- The Phil Collins / Whitney Houston monologues to terrified women.
- The realtor scene at the end.

**Easy to miss** — The film is funny. Mary Harron directs it as a comedy of male humiliation. The horror works because the satire works first.

**The test** — Did the murders happen?

**Connections** — *Fight Club*, *The Wolf of Wall Street*, *Joker*.

## Drive (2011)

**What it's really about** — Genre as emotional register. The Driver doesn't speak because he is constructed from movie grammar — he is the silent western hero stranded in an LA crime film. The love story is the film's only sincere idiom; everything else is choreography.

**Key scenes**
- The opening getaway, masterclass in tension without speed.
- The elevator kiss / kill.
- The motel-shootout aftermath in the diner.
- The final knife-and-jacket exchange with Bernie Rose.

**Easy to miss** — The scorpion jacket. He becomes the scorpion of the fable he tells — the one whose nature is to sting even his benefactors. The film's tragedy is that he knows it.

**The test** — Why is the Driver silent?

**Connections** — *Taxi Driver*, *Joker*, *No Country for Old Men*.

## Oldboy (2003, Park Chan-wook)

**What it's really about** — Vengeance as a closed loop. Oh Dae-su's quest to find out *why* he was imprisoned is the imprisonment. The film argues that the desire to know who hurt you is a hook the hurter is still using.

**Key scenes**
- The hammer-corridor one-shot.
- The eating-octopus scene.
- The hypnosis revelations.
- The tongue-cutting at the end.

**Easy to miss** — The film is structured as a Greek tragedy with the audience as oracle: we are told from early that there is a monstrous truth beneath, and we keep hoping the protagonist will stop digging. He doesn't, and that's the point.

**The test** — Why does Lee Woo-jin commit suicide after his revenge succeeds?

**Connections** — *Memories of Murder*, *Prisoners*, *Se7en*.

## No Country for Old Men (2007)

**What it's really about** — Evil as weather. Chigurh is not a villain — he is the new climate. Bell, the sheriff, can't even understand what he's policing anymore. The film argues that the moral imagination of the West has run out, and the men who remember the old rules can only retire.

**Key scenes**
- The coin toss at the gas station.
- Moss's death — offscreen, abrupt, denying us the showdown.
- Chigurh's car accident — the only moment chance touches him back.
- Bell's final dream-monologue at the kitchen table.

**Easy to miss** — Moss isn't the protagonist. Bell is. The film ends inside his retirement, his impotence, his dream of his father carrying fire forward in the dark — a torch he is now too old to carry.

**The test** — Why does the film deny us Moss's death scene?

**Connections** — *There Will Be Blood*, *Se7en*, *Memories of Murder*.

## Se7en (1995)

**What it's really about** — Mills doesn't lose because he's a worse cop than Somerset; he loses because he still believes the world rewards anger with justice. The film systematically dismantles the catharsis the genre exists to deliver.

**Key scenes**
- "What's in the box?"
- Somerset's library research scene scored to Bach.
- The interrogation of John Doe in the car.
- "Ernest Hemingway once wrote, 'The world is a fine place and worth fighting for.' I agree with the second part."

**Easy to miss** — John Doe is a craftsman. The film is shot like a procedural but cut like a religious text — Doe wrote the script, Mills enacts it. Mills's "Wrath" is John Doe's most successful sermon.

**The test** — Whose movie does Doe make Mills star in?

**Connections** — *The Silence of the Lambs*, *Prisoners*, *Memories of Murder*.

---

## The Silence of the Lambs (1991)

**What it's really about** — A young woman walks into men's gazes — Lecter's, Crawford's, Gumb's, every man in every elevator — and survives by understanding what each of them wants from her. The horror genre as workplace drama.

**Key scenes**
- Clarice descending into Lecter's hall the first time, the long approach.
- The autopsy with the death's-head moth.
- "Quid pro quo, Clarice."
- The night-vision basement finale.

**Easy to miss** — Demme keeps cutting to Foster's POV with men staring directly into the camera. The film is making you, the viewer, complicit in the gaze that Clarice has to navigate every day.

**The test** — Why does Lecter respect Clarice?

**Connections** — *Se7en*, *Prisoners*, *Memories of Murder*, *Mindhunter*-adjacent.

---

## Memories of Murder (2003, Bong Joon-ho)

**What it's really about** — A national failure in the form of a procedural. The detectives can't solve the case because the country in the 80s under dictatorship doesn't actually have the institutional capacity to handle truth. The film's despair is political, not narrative.

**Key scenes**
- The opening field with the ditch and the kids.
- The interrogation-room beating that produces the wrong confession.
- The DNA result from America that proves nothing.
- The final shot — Detective Park staring directly into the camera, decades later.

**Easy to miss** — The film is based on a true unsolved case (since solved in 2019, after the film). When it premiered, the killer was still out there, and the final stare into the camera was directed at him.

**The test** — Why does Park stare at us at the end?

**Connections** — *Se7en*, *Zodiac*, *Prisoners*.

---

## Prisoners (2013)

**What it's really about** — How quickly a moral man becomes a torturer when his daughter is taken. The film withholds judgment so you have to render it yourself. Loki is the procedural counterweight; Keller is the moral test.

**Key scenes**
- The Lord's Prayer recited while Keller boils Alex.
- The bloody-rabbit-mask reveal.
- The maze drawings.
- The whistle in the final frame.

**Easy to miss** — The film is about religion. Keller is a survivalist Catholic who fails his own catechism. The final whistle is grace arriving for him only after he has stopped praying.

**The test** — Is Keller saved at the end?

**Connections** — *Se7en*, *Mystic River*-adjacent, *Memories of Murder*.
