# Biographies & True Stories

How does a real life turn into a story? These films choose what to keep, what to invent, what to dramatize, and the choices reveal the filmmakers' theories of personhood at least as much as the subjects'.

---

## Notorious (Conor McGregor documentary, 2017)

**What it's really about** — A pre-fame portrait of working-class Dublin and its escape routes, told through the rise of an athlete who was never going to take the polite version of any of them. The film is more interesting before McGregor wins; the second half slips into hagiography.

**Key scenes**
- The early Dublin gym footage.
- The Aldo callout.
- The first championship fight.
- The post-Aldo monologue.

**Easy to miss** — The documentary was made before McGregor's later legal and personal controversies. Watching it now requires holding the early myth and the later man simultaneously — the film cannot anymore stand alone.

**The test** — What does the film not tell you?

**Connections** — *Look at Me: XXXTentacion*, *Marty Supreme*, *Joe Rogan: Triggered*.

## Look at Me: XXXTentacion (2022)

**What it's really about** — A short, violent life and the cultural machinery that grows around someone with an audience and unprocessed trauma. The doc is unusually clear-eyed about the abuse allegations against him, refusing to choose between "monster" and "victim" as descriptors. He was both, and the film says so.

**Key scenes**
- The early SoundCloud bedroom recordings.
- The girlfriend's testimony.
- The Florida prison-bus rant.
- The funeral.

**Easy to miss** — The film is a study in the way the music industry processes young Black trauma into product, and how rapidly the product can outrun the person. He was 20.

**The test** — Whose grief is the film willing to honor?

**Connections** — *Notorious*, *Dahmer* (counter-text on representational ethics), *Joker*.

## Miss Americana (2020)

**What it's really about** — Less Taylor Swift's career and more the radicalization of a celebrity-pleaser. The film tracks her decision to stop being "the good one" and start naming the costs (eating disorder, sexual assault trial, political silence). It's a documentary about a performance ending.

**Key scenes**
- The 2009 VMAs interrupted speech.
- The trial-prep footage.
- The "I want to wear pink and want to talk about politics" decision.
- The "All Too Well" creative process.

**Easy to miss** — The film is a managed text. It is not vérité — it is co-authored. That doesn't make it false; it makes it a different kind of object: a self-portrait, mediated.

**The test** — What does the film leave out?

**Connections** — *The Social Network*, *Notorious*, *Look at Me*.

## Dahmer — Monster: The Jeffrey Dahmer Story (2022)

**What it's really about** — A serial killer drama that is, in part, an indictment of the institutional failures that let Dahmer keep killing — Milwaukee police, neighbors, family, the systemic dismissal of complaints from queer Black men. The show has been criticized for retraumatizing victims' families; reading those critiques is part of watching it responsibly.

**Key scenes**
- The Konerak Sinthasomphone return-to-Dahmer scene with the police.
- Glenda Cleveland's repeated, ignored calls.
- The trial speeches by victims' families.
- The Dahmer-prison interview footage.

**Easy to miss** — The show's most damning material is procedural, not gory: the records of complaints filed and ignored. The horror is institutional racism and homophobia, made visible by Dahmer rather than authored by him.

**The test** — Why does the show give Glenda Cleveland an entire episode?

**Connections** — *The Good Nurse*, *The Silence of the Lambs*, *Memories of Murder*.

## The Good Nurse (2022)

**What it's really about** — A serial-killer hospital drama that is also a hospital-administration drama. Cullen got away with killing patients across multiple hospitals because each institution chose silence and reference-letters over investigation. The film's actual subject is liability law.

**Key scenes**
- The first patient code blue.
- Amy's at-home cardiomyopathy reality check.
- The detective interviews with the hospital lawyers.
- The diner confession.

**Easy to miss** — Eddie's quiet kindness to Amy and her kids is not redeeming detail. The film is showing how compartmentalization works in real life. He was a friend and a killer at the same time, and that is the actual horror.

**The test** — Why does the film resist a "monstrous" portrayal of Cullen?

**Connections** — *Dahmer*, *The Silence of the Lambs*, *Memories of Murder*.

## Dallas Buyers Club (2013)

**What it's really about** — A homophobic Texas electrician with HIV who finds out the FDA's drug-approval process is killing him faster than the disease. The film's politics are libertarian-coded but its practical alliance with queer Houston is the actual emotional content. It is a film about being saved by the people you were raised to despise.

**Key scenes**
- The diagnosis.
- The first Mexico run.
- Ron and Rayon's slow alliance.
- The closing tribute card.

**Easy to miss** — The film has been critiqued for centering a straight white man inside a queer health crisis. That critique is fair and worth holding alongside the film. Watch it knowing that history is more crowded than the script admits.

**The test** — Whose story is the film telling and whose is it eliding?

**Connections** — *The Pursuit of Happyness*, *A Beautiful Mind*, *We Live in Time*.

## The Pursuit of Happyness (2006)

**What it's really about** — Surface: a homeless father pulls himself into a stockbroker job. Underneath: the brutal arithmetic of American social mobility — that one man's success requires conditions vanishingly rare and that the film's "uplift" is structurally a cautionary tale about how badly the safety net failed.

**Key scenes**
- The bone-density-scanner sales calls.
- The subway-bathroom night.
- "I'm the type of person, if you ask me a question and I don't know the answer, I'm gonna tell you that I don't know."
- The applause-line ending.

**Easy to miss** — The film never says what happens to most fathers in Chris's situation. The implicit answer is "their kids end up in foster care." The triumph requires you not to think about the median outcome.

**The test** — What conditions made Chris's story possible?

**Connections** — *Forrest Gump*, *It's a Wonderful Life*, *Capernaum*.

## The Man Who Knew Infinity (2015)

**What it's really about** — Ramanujan in Cambridge, watched by Hardy. The film argues that mathematical genius is also a faith claim, and that the British rationalist tradition was unprepared for an Indian mystic who simply saw the truth. The clash is not individual; it's epistemic.

**Key scenes**
- The first letters from Madras.
- Ramanujan's refusal to "prove" his theorems Hardy's way.
- The illness in Cambridge.
- Hardy at Trinity at the end.

**Easy to miss** — The film handles Hardy's intellectual loneliness as carefully as Ramanujan's racial isolation. Hardy, the godless rationalist, is moved by a man whose math is divine in a way Hardy can almost — but not quite — feel.

**The test** — What does Hardy lose when Ramanujan dies?

**Connections** — *A Beautiful Mind*, *Whiplash*, *The Theory of Everything*.

## Marty Supreme (2025)

**What it's really about** — A sports biopic of an unlikely subject (table tennis) that reads as a meditation on hunger and self-mythology in the Eisenhower-era American Jewish community. The film's claim is that obsession at the small scale (a basement, a 9-foot table) is the same disease as ambition at the large scale.

**Key scenes**
- The basement-bar scenes.
- The first international tournament.
- The personal cost — relationships sidelined for the table.

**Easy to miss** — The film is part of a wave of recent biopics interested in marginal sports as windows on midcentury American identity (cf. *King Richard*, *Nyad*).

**The test** — What is "supreme" actually a pun on?

**Connections** — *Whiplash*, *Notorious*, *The Wolf of Wall Street*, *Dangal*.

## Двадцать шесть дней из жизни Достоевского (1981)

**What it's really about** — Dostoevsky during the writing of *The Gambler*, dictating to a young stenographer who later becomes his wife. The film is a chamber piece about a great writer at his most desperate — broke, dying, behind on contracts — and the woman who organized him into being able to finish.

**Key scenes**
- The first morning of dictation.
- The roulette flashbacks.
- The proposal.
- The closing lines being typed.

**Easy to miss** — The film positions Anna Grigorievna as the actual protagonist. She is the system that makes Dostoevsky possible; the film's argument is that the canonical Dostoevsky we read was largely managed into existence by her.

**The test** — Whose 26 days are these?

**Connections** — *Anna Karenina*, *Crime and Punishment*, *Brothers Karamazov*, *Master and Margarita*.

## Joe Rogan: Triggered (2012)

**What it's really about** — A pre-mainstream Rogan special, useful now mostly as a time capsule. The set is rougher and less ideologically consolidated than the Rogan of the late 2010s. Watching it after-the-fact is an exercise in tracking what happened to a comic between then and now.

**Key scenes**
- The opening fitness-and-DMT bits.
- The political-conspiracy material.

**Easy to miss** — The 2012 Rogan was less politically aligned than the post-2017 Rogan. Whether that's growth, drift, or radicalization is a question the special itself doesn't answer.

**The test** — How does this set predict the next ten years?

**Connections** — *The Social Dilemma*, *Don't Look Up*, *Idiocracy*.

## Brain on Fire (2016)

**What it's really about** — A real medical mystery — anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis presenting as psychiatric illness — and the way young women's reports of being unwell are systematically dismissed. The film is partly a horror film about diagnosis itself.

**Key scenes**
- The first symptoms at work.
- The escalating misdiagnoses.
- The neurologist's clock-drawing test.
- The recovery.

**Easy to miss** — The film is based on the patient's own memoir, written after recovery. The first-person framing is doing the work — it is asserting the credibility of a young woman the medical system gaslit.

**The test** — Why does the diagnosis arrive only when a particular doctor listens?

**Connections** — *Adolescence*, *13 Reasons Why*, *Babygirl* (counter-text on women being heard).
