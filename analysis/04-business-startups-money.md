## The Almanack of Naval Ravikant — ed. Eric Jorgenson

**Thesis.** Wealth is not money — it's assets that earn while you sleep — and the path to it is *specific knowledge* (skills you can't be trained for) deployed with *leverage* (code, capital, media) and accountability under your own name. Happiness is a separate, equally trainable skill.

**Key ideas.**
- *Specific knowledge* — knowledge that can't be taught in a school; the kind that's a hobby to you and work to others.
- *Leverage* = labor + capital + code/media. Code and media are permissionless and infinitely scalable.
- *Play long-term games with long-term people* — almost every reward (financial, relational, intellectual) is the byproduct of compounding loyalty.
- *Earn with your mind, not your time* — selling hours has no leverage.
- *Happiness is a skill* — peace from desire, peace from thoughts, peace from action.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll keep selling time for money, optimize for credentials over leverage, and arrive at "success" with no compounding asset and no peace.

**Test question.** Name your specific knowledge in one sentence and the form of leverage you're applying to it. If you can't, that's the work.

**Connections.** Spiritual descendant of *Hackers & Painters* (Graham). Philosophical sibling: *Almanack*'s happiness chapters echo *Siddhartha*, *Tuesdays with Morrie*, Stoicism. Counterweight: *Lessons of History* (Durant — most luck and history are not under your control), *Black Swan/Antifragile* angle. The "build your own thing" axis runs through *Zero to One*, *Hackers & Painters*, *Anthology of Balaji*.

## How to Get Rich — Naval Ravikant

*(podcast/Twitter compilation, 40 tweets and elaborations)*

**Thesis.** A condensed, tactical version of the *Almanack*'s wealth thread: wealth-creation is a learnable, ethical pursuit if you choose the right business model (equity in something that scales) and avoid the seductions that destroy compounding (envy, debt, status games).

**Key ideas.**
- Seek *equity*, not wages.
- Pick partners with *intelligence, energy, and integrity* — missing the third is fatal.
- *There are no get-rich-quick schemes; that's just someone else getting rich off you.*
- Status games are zero-sum; wealth games are positive-sum. Don't confuse them.
- Read what you love until you love to read.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll work hard at status when you think you're working hard at money, and burn years winning the wrong game.

**Test question.** In your current pursuit, are you building equity or trading hours? And which game (status or wealth) are you actually playing?

**Connections.** Companion to the *Almanack*. Reinforces *Hackers & Painters*, *Zero to One*, *Anthology of Balaji*. Counter: *Богатый папа бедный папа* (cruder), *The Richest Man in Babylon* (older, gentler).

## Богатый папа бедный папа — Robert Kiyosaki

*(Rich Dad, Poor Dad)*

**Thesis.** Schools train employees, not owners; the difference between rich and poor isn't income but the *side of the cash-flow statement* you live on. Assets put money into your pocket; liabilities take it out — and most middle-class "assets" (a mortgaged house, a car loan) are actually liabilities.

**Key ideas.**
- The cash-flow quadrant: Employee, Self-employed, Business owner, Investor.
- Buy assets first, lifestyle second.
- Financial literacy is the missing subject; you have to teach yourself.
- Fear of failure keeps people in jobs they hate.
- Work to learn, not (only) to earn — early in your career, choose roles for the lessons.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll keep upgrading your lifestyle in lockstep with your income and call yourself middle-class your whole life.

**Test question.** List your top three monthly expenses. Which of them is feeding an asset, and which is feeding a liability?

**Connections.** Cruder, more aggressive cousin of *The Richest Man in Babylon*. Same family as Naval (*Almanack*, *How to Get Rich*) but less elegant. Counter-cultural critique: *The Communist Manifesto*, *Understanding Power* (Chomsky), *Skin in the Game* (Taleb on hidden risk transfer).

> Note: Kiyosaki's biographical claims and specific advice are widely disputed; the cash-flow framing is the durable contribution. The rest, treat as starter mythology, not gospel.

## The Richest Man in Babylon — George S. Clason

**Thesis.** A handful of ancient, boring rules, dramatized through Babylonian parables, are sufficient to build wealth: pay yourself first, control your spending, make your money work, guard against loss, own your home, ensure income for old age. The whole "personal finance" genre is a footnote.

**Key ideas.**
- *Pay yourself first* — save at least 10% before paying anyone else.
- Live below your means; rising income should not become rising expenses.
- Make your gold work — money earning money is the second worker on your team.
- Guard your principal; protect against loss before reaching for return.
- Get advice from those who deal with the matter at hand, not those who don't.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll keep treating personal finance as complicated to justify not doing the simple things.

**Test question.** What percentage of last month's income did you save *before* spending? If it's zero, the rest of your reading on money is theater.

**Connections.** Older, gentler grandparent of *Rich Dad Poor Dad* and *Almanack of Naval*. Unironic moral framing connects to *Не Грусти!* and Quranic ethics around debt and *zakah*. Counter: *Skin in the Game*, *Antifragile* — risk is more subtle than pay-yourself-first suggests.

## Zero to One — Peter Thiel

**Thesis.** Real progress is *vertical* — going from 0 to 1 by inventing something genuinely new — not *horizontal* (copying what works at larger scale). Monopoly, not competition, is the right goal of a startup; the question every founder must answer is "what important truth do very few people agree with you on?"

**Key ideas.**
- Competition is for losers — sustainable profits live in monopoly, not commodity.
- *Last-mover advantage* — be the last meaningful entrant in a category.
- The *contrarian question* — "what do you believe that almost no one agrees with?"
- *Definite optimism* (we can plan and build the future) vs. *indefinite optimism* (it'll be fine, somehow). The West has slid to indefinite.
- Sales matters; founders who think "the product sells itself" are losing.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll spend your career in horizontal progress (incremental better-but-cheaper) and never attempt the harder, rarer game of building something new.

**Test question.** Answer Thiel's contrarian question with one concrete sentence. If you can't, you don't yet have a startup thesis.

**Connections.** Sibling of *Hackers & Painters*, *Anthology of Balaji*, the Naval canon. Counter: *Lean Startup* (incrementalism is the right *method*, even if monopoly is the right *goal*). Historical frame: *The Lessons of History*, *The Beginning of Infinity*.

## The Lean Startup — Eric Ries

**Thesis.** Most startups fail because they build things nobody wants; the cure is *validated learning* — running cheap experiments to test hypotheses about customers before scaling production. Build–measure–learn is the unit of work.

**Key ideas.**
- *MVP* — the smallest experiment that produces validated learning, not the smallest product.
- *Pivot* vs. *persevere* — change the hypothesis, not the metric.
- *Vanity metrics* (totals, downloads) hide what's working; *actionable metrics* (cohort behavior) reveal it.
- Innovation accounting — measure progress *toward* a sustainable business, not just activity.
- Speed of iteration > size of release.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll spend a year building before testing, fall in love with the product, and discover too late nobody wanted it.

**Test question.** What's the next hypothesis you can test in your project for less than a week of work, and what would falsify it?

**Connections.** Methodological complement to *Zero to One* (Thiel: pick the right game; Ries: how to test inside it). Sibling: *Inspired*, *Building Large Scale Web Apps*. Cousin: scientific method as in *The Beginning of Infinity*, *The Meaning of It All*.

## Inspired — Marty Cagan

**Thesis.** Successful tech products come from empowered, cross-functional teams given problems to solve, not roadmaps of features to ship; the product manager's job is to discover what's valuable, usable, feasible, and viable — *before* engineering builds it.

**Key ideas.**
- *Discovery* (figure out what to build) is a separate craft from *delivery* (build it).
- The four risks: value, usability, feasibility, viability — every product idea has to clear all four.
- Empowered teams beat feature factories; outcomes beat outputs.
- The PM is the *CEO of the product* — accountable, not just facilitative.
- Roadmaps of features are a project-management lie; outcome roadmaps are honest.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll join or run feature-factory teams and confuse shipping with progress.

**Test question.** For the last feature your team shipped, can you state the *outcome* it was meant to move and whether it moved?

**Connections.** Sibling of *Lean Startup*. Engineering side: *Building Large Scale Web Apps*, *Designing Data-Intensive Applications*, *A Philosophy of Software Design*. Strategic frame: *Zero to One*, *Almanack*.

## Hackers & Painters — Paul Graham

**Thesis.** Programming is more like painting than like physics — a creative craft of taste, iteration, and beauty, performed by misfits whose social marginality is partly the source of their leverage. Startups are a way for the most productive painters to capture the value of their work.

**Key ideas.**
- The best programmers think in *abstractions* fluently — Lisp is the canonical example of letting the language match the thought.
- *Schools as prisons* — the social brutality of high school is structural, not personal.
- Wealth ≠ money; wealth is what people want, and you create it by making things they want.
- Startups are *compressed careers* — you do twenty years of work in four, in exchange for accepting the variance.
- *What you can't say* — be aware of the orthodoxies of your time so you can think.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll let the surrounding culture decide what's worth working on and quietly absorb its current taboos.

**Test question.** What's one belief you hold that you'd be afraid to say out loud at work? Why are you afraid?

**Connections.** Foundational for the Naval/Balaji axis (*Almanack*, *How to Get Rich*, *Anthology of Balaji*). Engineering taste: *A Philosophy of Software Design*, *Surely You're Joking*. Counter-frame: *Communist Manifesto*, *Understanding Power*.

## The Anthology of Balaji — ed. Eric Jorgenson

**Thesis.** The future will be increasingly built by *network states* — internet-native communities that earn legitimacy and capability before sovereignty — and individuals who develop tech, capital, and media leverage simultaneously will accumulate disproportionate optionality. Balaji's frame stitches biology, software, finance, and political theory into a single forecasting model.

**Key ideas.**
- *Network state* — start with a moral cause, build a community online, earn capital, acquire territory, seek diplomatic recognition.
- The dollar reserve era is ending; commodity, crypto, and software stores of value are rising.
- Truth, time, and trust become network primitives — verifiable on-chain.
- *Founders > heads of state* in many domains today; institutions are losing competence faster than legitimacy.
- Compound the three forms of capital: *cognitive, financial, social*.

**Dangerous to forget.** You'll think of the next decade in 20th-century categories (jobs, nations, careers) when the categories themselves are being rewritten.

**Test question.** Sketch a network state you'd join in one sentence — moral cause, online community, capital base, territory thesis. The point is not feasibility; the point is whether you can think in this frame.

**Connections.** Sibling of *Almanack* and *How to Get Rich*. Argument-partner with *Communist Manifesto*, *Understanding Power*, *Skin in the Game*. Engineering substrate: *Hackers & Painters*, *Beginning of Infinity*.

> Note: This is a compilation, not a single thesis from Balaji directly; weight individual claims accordingly.

## World-Class Warehousing and Material Handling — Edward Frazelle

**Thesis.** Warehousing is not a cost center to be minimized but a competitive system whose design — slotting, picking, layout, IT — drives margin in any physical-goods business. World-class warehouses are engineered, not inherited.

**Key ideas.**
- Receiving, put-away, storage, picking, packing, shipping — six processes, each with its own metrics.
- ABC analysis — most movement comes from a small fraction of SKUs; slot accordingly.
- Travel time is the dominant cost in picking; layout decisions compound.
- Automation pays off only after process is right; mechanizing chaos amplifies chaos.
- The warehouse is a real-time information system that happens to also move boxes.

**Dangerous to forget.** If you operate a physical-goods business, you'll keep treating warehousing as plumbing and let your competitors out-engineer you on margin.

**Test question.** What's the slotting principle that would cut travel time the most in the warehouse you know best?

**Connections.** Engineering-mindset cousin to *A Philosophy of Software Design* and *Designing Data-Intensive Applications* — same discipline, different substrate. Operational sibling of *Inspired*, *Lean Startup*. Strategic frame: *Skin in the Game* (operators see what consultants don't).

## Across this file

A useful map:

- **Wealth philosophy**: Naval (×2), Kiyosaki, Babylon — *what is wealth and how does it compound*.
- **Building**: Thiel, Ries, Cagan, Graham, Balaji — *what to build and how to test it*.
- **Operations**: Frazelle — *how to actually run the system once it exists*.

The honest order to read in: *Babylon → Naval → Kiyosaki → Thiel → Ries → Cagan → Graham → Balaji → Frazelle*. The earlier books are about *not making yourself poor*; the later ones are about *building leverage*.
